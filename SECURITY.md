# Security Policy

Silocat is a zero-knowledge product: the security claims *are* the product. If
those claims are wrong, we want to know before your users do.

## Reporting a vulnerability

Email **security@silo.cat** with:

- what you found and where (file, endpoint, or a URL),
- how to reproduce it, ideally a minimal proof of concept,
- what you think the impact is.

Please **do not** open a public issue for anything security-relevant, and please
don't post it publicly before we've had a chance to fix it.

If you want to encrypt the report, say so in a first email with no details and
we'll send you a key.

### What to expect

| | |
|---|---|
| First response | within 3 working days |
| Triage and severity assessment | within 7 days |
| Fix for a confirmed critical issue | as fast as we can, and we'll keep you updated |
| Credit | your name or handle in the release notes, unless you'd rather stay anonymous |

We're a small team, so timelines are honest rather than aspirational. If you
don't hear back in 3 days, email again: it means something went wrong on our
end, not that we're ignoring you.

We don't run a paid bug bounty. We can't compete with the companies that do, and
we'd rather promise nothing than promise money we can't pay.

## Scope

**In scope**

- Anything that breaks the zero-knowledge property: the server learning a
  plaintext file, a password, or a derived key.
- Weaknesses in the client-side crypto: key derivation (Argon2 parameters),
  chunk encryption (XChaCha20-Poly1305), nonce or salt handling, the share
  password generator.
- Authentication and session handling, including the anonymous browser-key flow.
- Authorisation: reading, modifying or deleting another account's files,
  folders or shares.
- The admin surface: reaching any `/admin` route without a valid admin session.
- Share links: guessing a token, bypassing a link password, exceeding a
  one-time download limit, or accessing an expired link.
- Server-side injection, SSRF, or anything that gets code execution.
- Billing flows that let someone grant themselves storage they didn't pay for.

**Out of scope**

- Anything requiring a compromised device, a malicious browser extension, or
  physical access. We cannot defend against an attacker who already owns the
  client.
- The obvious consequence of the design: lose your password, lose your file. We
  cannot recover it and that is deliberate, not a bug.
- Rate limiting on endpoints that only cost the attacker their own time.
- Missing security headers with no demonstrated impact.
- Reports generated by a scanner with no manual verification, and "best
  practice" findings with no exploit path.
- Social engineering of our team or our users.
- Self-hosted instances that are misconfigured (secrets unset, admin exposed to
  the internet). Tell us if our defaults or docs led you there, though: that
  part *is* in scope.

## What we guarantee, and what we don't

We hold ciphertext. We do not hold, and cannot recover, your decryption
passwords. We do hold metadata: file sizes, timestamps, MIME types, content
hashes, and the account or browser key an upload belongs to. If your threat
model includes "the server learns that a 4 GB file was uploaded on Tuesday",
Silocat does not solve that.

Privacy is not lawlessness. We comply with valid legal process to the extent we
can, which is limited by the fact that we cannot read file contents.

## Self-hosting

If you run your own instance, you own its security. Two things bite people
most often:

1. **`ADMIN_COMMUNICATION_SECRET` unset.** This is intentional: the admin surface fails closed
   and is unreachable until you set it. Generate a real one
   (`openssl rand -hex 32`) and never reuse it across environments. There is no
   admin account and no login page: the secret *is* the credential, sent as
   `X-Admin-Secret`.
2. **Exposing `/admin` publicly.** The bundled nginx config returns 404 for
   `/admin` on the public zone and expects the admin surface on a separate,
   access-controlled hostname. Keep it that way.

Never reuse the example values in `env.selfhost.example`. They are placeholders,
not defaults.
